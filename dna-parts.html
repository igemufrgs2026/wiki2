Overview

This page presents the DNA parts used to build our biosensor, along with a brief description of the role each one plays within the genetic circuit. The system was designed to detect the herbicide 2,4-D through a regulatory mechanism coupled to an aptamer-based fluorescent reporter, and the tables below detail each component of the construct.

Our biosensor draws on the natural CadR/pCadA regulatory system from Bradyrhizobium sp. HW13. In this design, CadR is constitutively expressed under the strong J23119 promoter and normally binds to the CadA promoter (pCadA), repressing transcription of the downstream cassette. When 2,4-D is present, it induces a conformational change in CadR that relieves this repression, allowing transcription to proceed.

Rather than relying on a conventional protein reporter, the system uses an RNA-based one composed of RiboJ and the 3WJdB aptamer. RiboJ processes and insulates the reporter transcript, while 3WJdB binds the fluorescent dye DFHBI-1T to generate a detectable signal. Combined with a cell-free expression platform, this design allows the construct to translate 2,4-D detection directly into RNA production and fluorescence — a fast and practical sensing strategy.
