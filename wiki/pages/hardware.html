Our Hardware

A fundamental part of our environmental biosensor is the ability to reliably quantify the fluorescence produced by the biological construct in response to the presence of 2,4-D. To address this challenge, we developed a compact, portable device that combines fluorescence detection, temperature control, data processing, and user interaction in a single platform.

Our goal was not simply to build a fluorescence reader, but to develop a system that could be portable, affordable, autonomous, and easy to operate. The device guides the user through the measurement process, maintains the conditions required for the biological reaction, detects the resulting fluorescence, and converts the measurement into an interpretable result.

The system is built around four main functions:

Fluorescence Detection

The biological construct produces a fluorescent signal when exposed to 2,4-D. The device illuminates the sample with blue light, exciting the fluorescent molecules, and measures the resulting green fluorescence with an optical sensor.

A filter separates the excitation and emission wavelengths, preventing the blue excitation light from dominating the fluorescence measurement. This allows the device to distinguish the signal produced by the biological reaction from the light used to excite it.

Controlled Reaction Environment

Fluorescence measurements depend not only on the optical signal but also on the conditions in which the biological reaction takes place. The device therefore incorporates an automated incubation system that maintains the sample at 37°C during the reaction.

Temperature is monitored continuously, and the system automatically controls the heating process. Safety limits are also incorporated to prevent excessive temperatures and protect both the sample and the equipment.

Autonomous Operation and User Interface

The device was designed to operate without requiring an external computer, server, or internet connection. It creates its own local Wi-Fi network, allowing the user to access the control interface from a smartphone or computer through a web browser.

The interface provides real-time information about the measurement and the state of the device. It also handles the main stages of the measurement workflow, including incubation, signal processing, calibration, and system diagnostics.

The device can additionally communicate measurement results to our 2,4-D monitoring platform, allowing users to share measurements and contribute to a broader picture of herbicide occurrence across different locations.

Portable and Accessible Design

The device is powered by a rechargeable battery pack and continuously monitors its available charge, allowing measurements to be performed without access to a laboratory power supply.

We also prioritized the use of commercially available and relatively inexpensive components. The estimated cost of the complete hardware is approximately R$280 (~US$55), making the platform substantially more accessible than conventional laboratory-based analytical approaches such as HPLC, which costs thousands of Brazilian reais.

By combining biological sensing with accessible electronics and automated analysis, our hardware is intended to bring environmental monitoring closer to the communities and users who need it.
