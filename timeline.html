More than a project, our story is one of resilience, adaptation, and collective growth. Every obstacle became a chance to learn, and every setback reinforced our commitment to science, education, and teamwork.

Late 2023: The Spark

Our team’s journey began in late 2023, inspired by conversations with former iGEM UFRGS participants (the 2019 Team) who showed us that building a synthetic biology team in southern Brazil was possible. What started as a small group driven by curiosity soon became a shared commitment to bringing iGEM back to UFRGS.

Taking part in the iGEM experience always seemed like an unreachable dream to me from the very beginning when I first heard the 2019 team talking about their experience. In that presentation, I could see my friend’s eyes lighting up at the possibility of us participating too, but my realistic side could only think about how much it would cost to take part in an international competition of this scale.

Since then, my friends and I have been working together and building our team from scratch, with the help of some professors, but mostly by putting our own hands to work. At first we were very lost about what problem we would solve with our Synthetic Biology skills, focusing mainly on environmental problems since there are many in Brazil. But then we talked with some seniors, professors, and friends and we heard about a problem caused by a herbicide drifting from soy farms that directly occurs in our state, but also in other parts of the country.

Looking back, it has been two really hard years of research, planning, hard work, and good laughs. In the end, for me, the greatest achievement throughout all of this was finally seeing our team, our name, officially part of the competition. It has definitely been a life-changing experience that has taught me so much about perseverance and scientific collaboration.

Isabelli Seiler Student Leader & Founding Member

Early 2024: A New Direction

Along the way, we faced challenges that repeatedly forced us to rethink our plans. The cancellation of the iGEM Design League in 2024 could have ended the project before it truly began, but instead, it strengthened our determination. We chose to continue, setting a new, highly ambitious goal: reaching the iGEM Grand Jamboree.

When the iGEM Design League was unexpectedly cancelled, it felt like the rug was pulled out from under us. For a moment, we thought the dream was over. But witnessing the students' immediate reaction—choosing to pivot and aim directly for the Grand Jamboree instead of giving up—showed me the true character of this group. It was no longer just an extracurricular academic exercise; it became a genuine mission driven by resilience.

Prof. Diego Bonatto Primary PI

Mid 2024 - 2025: Rebuilding & Resilience

Soon after, the devastating floods that struck Rio Grande do Sul interrupted university activities for months and directly affected several team members. Despite the uncertainty and the profound impact on our community, we gradually regrouped, resumed our meetings remotely, and rebuilt our momentum.

Throughout 2024 and 2025, the team continued to grow. We welcomed new members through a second recruitment process and participated in several key events that shaped both the project and our development. Attending the 1st Brazilian Congress on Synthetic Biology placed us within the broader national synbio community, while our involvement in public hearings, governmental working groups, and multi-stakeholder discussions on 2,4-D contamination anchored our project's social relevance.

Joining the team during such a turbulent time—right as the university was rebuilding from the historical floods—was incredibly intense. We found a group that had just been through immense hardship but was more determined than ever. Seeing the passion everyone had for the 2,4Detector project made us realize we were stepping into something deeply meaningful. We brought fresh energy, but we inherited a foundation of pure resilience.

Mariana & Sofia Student Members (2024 Recruitment)

2025: Making it Happen

Financial limitations repeatedly forced us to adapt our timelines. After taking the hard decision to postpone our official participation from 2025 to 2026, we redirected our energy toward building a sustainable financial future for the team.

We organized the 1st Gaúcho Symposium on Biomolecular Engineering, developed the online course Synthetic Biology for Beginners, secured critical support from biotechnology companies and academic institutions, and created grassroots fundraising initiatives—from educational activities to selling brownies across the campus—to keep the project alive.

2025 was a life-changing step in our team's journey. Before that, raising enough money to cover all the competition fees seemed impossible, especially considering the financial reality of our university and the recent regional crisis. However, by the end of the year, after selling countless brownies, receiving many donations, and raising funds through our symposium registrations, we finally saw the light at the end of the tunnel. The feeling of finally having everything wrapped up for the competition was indescribable!

Camilla Brückmann Student Leader & Finance

iGEM, Here We Go.

By the end of 2025, after two years of persistence, planning, and relentless hard work, we had gathered the resources, the wet lab experience, and the confidence needed to finally bring our vision to life.
